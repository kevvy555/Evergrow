# Engagement design

Evergrow aims for very high replayability through intrinsic play, mastery, curiosity and attachment rather than punishment, forced waiting, loss aversion, opaque loot odds or monetized pressure.

## Target mental loop

At most moments the player should feel at least one of these:

- **I am close** — primed pair, Bloom charge, wish completion or next discovery.
- **I can do this better** — delay for a Perfect Merge, preserve Flow, exploit weather or position a settlement intentionally.
- **Something could emerge** — Resonance cascade, Radiant birth, Wonder, Harmony district or weather opportunity.
- **Someone in my world cares** — a named settlement has a contextual wish.
- **This world is mine** — persistent names, Harmony identities, evolution choices and Journal history reflect this particular run.

## Why v0.5 moves from systems to attachment

v0.4 made the board tactically richer, but settlements were still mostly progression icons. Emotional attachment needs continuity, identity and visible feedback from the world itself.

v0.5 therefore adds a living-world layer that uses the same board rather than opening a separate management game.

## Persistent settlement identity

When a Village or higher settlement first appears it receives a deterministic name such as Willow, Ember or Sunmere. The name is stored on the entity and inherited as that settlement evolves.

Names matter because later wishes and Harmony celebrations reference the place directly. A request from **Willow** is more memorable than a generic objective panel, while requiring zero additional controls.

## Contextual settlement wishes

Once people exist, a settlement can occasionally ask for one simple action that is already part of normal play:

- grow nature beside the settlement;
- make a nearby merge;
- make a nearby Perfect Merge;
- collect a Life Spark.

Only one wish is active. There is no expiry timer and ignoring a wish has no punishment. Completion gives score, Bloom energy and Community Joy.

The intent is to create local purpose without converting the game into a quest checklist.

## Harmony districts

A settlement beside Tree/Grove-level nature becomes a permanent Harmony district. The identity survives future settlement merges.

This makes spatial coexistence valuable for three reasons at once:

1. it can complete a Garden wish;
2. it awards an immediate visible Harmony moment;
3. it permanently changes the settlement's identity.

This is stronger than another transient multiplier because the board remembers what the player built.

## Community Joy and Festivals

Every fulfilled wish adds Community Joy. Two fulfilled wishes trigger a short four-turn Festival.

Festival is deliberately short and non-destructive. Successful merge turns receive a modest bonus and extra Bloom energy while settlements visibly celebrate. There is no penalty when Festival ends.

This creates a second rhythm layered over Bloom:

`settlement appears → wish → fulfillment → another wish → Festival → calm`

The two rhythms arise from different player behavior, so they can occasionally overlap for memorable peaks without being permanently active.

## Weather as opportunity, not obstruction

Weather cycles deterministically after Nature is established and is separated by clear periods.

### Rain
Tapping beside existing nature can create a Tree instead of a Sprout. Rain encourages temporarily clustering growth around natural areas and helps Garden/Harmony play.

### Golden Hour
Merge score receives a deterministic bonus. This rewards players who save prepared merges for the short opportunity window.

### Starlight
Life Sparks collected under Starlight become Radiant. This gives Spark timing a visible strategic twist without adding random loot odds.

Weather never destroys pieces, blocks input or punishes the player for missing it.

## Day/night and living movement

Dawn/day/dusk/night are derived from tap count and affect presentation only. Roads appear between nearby settlements, tiny traffic visibly moves along them, settlement life motes shift each turn, weather overlays the board and Festivals add confetti.

These details are intentionally non-interactive. Their purpose is to make the world feel inhabited without turning citizens into units that must be managed.

## Reward time scales after v0.5

### Every tap
Immediate growth/feedback and subtle ambient movement.

### Several taps
Primed merges, Flow, contextual wishes and weather exploitation.

### Tens of taps
Bloom, Radiants, Harmony districts, settlement identity and Wonder discovery.

### Longer arcs
Named settlements evolve, wishes accumulate into Festivals, the Journal fills and permanent evolution perks produce increasingly distinct worlds.

## Synthetic pacing checkpoint

A 200-run random-input simulation over 300 taps found approximately:

- first Bloom around tap 37 median;
- first weather mood around tap 42 median;
- first Village/name/wish around tap 104 median;
- first Harmony district around tap 116 median;
- first fulfilled wish around tap 126 median;
- first Festival around tap 160 median after tuning Community Joy to two wishes.

Random input is intentionally inefficient. Deliberate human play should encounter settlement-layer systems earlier.

## Guardrails

- No forced ads or pay-to-win mechanics.
- No punishment for leaving the game.
- No real-time expiry on wishes, weather or earned progress.
- No opaque loot-box probability loop.
- No destructive streak loss.
- No extra control unless it creates more depth than complexity.
- Prefer visible board consequences and persistent identity over arbitrary currency inflation.
- Special modes must remain special; tune frequency rather than stacking permanent boosts.
